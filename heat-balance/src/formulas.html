Minimisation problem

At bath temperature T and pressure P the model finds the amounts ni of all species that minimise the total Gibbs energy while conserving every element. aij is the number of atoms of element j in species i and bj is the amount of element j fed. Ng and Ns are the total moles of gas and slag. Slag species are single-cation oxides.

Conditions at the minimum

The Lagrange multipliers of the element balances are the element potentials λj. At the minimum every species that is present has a chemical potential equal to the sum of its element potentials. A phase that is absent must not be able to lower G by forming. Activities and partial pressures are reported from the element potentials, so they are also defined for species and phases that are absent.

Newton iteration

The unknowns of each iteration are the element potentials πj, the change of each solution-phase total Δln Np, and the change of each pure-phase amount Δnc. The species corrections follow from them. This is the RAND method of White, Johnson and Dantzig as used in NASA CEA, extended to two solution phases. Trace species are also limited so that they cannot rise above a mole fraction of 10−4 in one step. After convergence a pure phase with ac > 1 is added, a pure phase with a negative amount is removed, and a solution phase with Qp > 1 is switched on.

Standard-state functions

Species data are NASA seven-coefficient polynomials. Enthalpies are absolute: the elements in their reference states at T0 = 298.15 K have zero enthalpy, so h°(T0) is the enthalpy of formation. Outside a fitted range, with Tb the nearest range limit, the heat capacity is held constant. ZnO uses a three-term heat capacity with a = 45.338, b = 7.289×10−3, c = −5.732×105 (J, mol, K). The FeO correction refers its activity to liquid iron oxide in equilibrium with iron.

Equilibria in the bath

These relations are not imposed. They follow from the conditions at the minimum and are the usual way to read the result. K is the equilibrium constant of the reaction written with pure-species standard states. The Equilibrium tab lists ΔrH°, ΔrG° and log K at the bath temperature. Aluminium reacts to completion, so its capacity is set by stoichiometry.

Feeds

The slag assay is converted to moles of slag species per kilogram, with w in wt% and f3 the ferric share of iron. The enthalpy of dry coal at T0 follows from its higher heating value and the enthalpies of its combustion products, with w the mass fractions of dry coal, md the dry mass and nw the moles of moisture. If the heating value is estimated, the Channiwala and Parikh correlation is used with the analysis in wt% dry and A the ash. Blast volume is at 0 °C and 101 325 Pa. fm is the metallic share of the aluminium feed.

Heat balance

The enthalpy of a stream or phase is the sum over its species. Because the activity coefficients do not depend on temperature, the slag has no excess enthalpy. The balance is written with total enthalpies, so heats of reaction are included without listing reactions. Qnet is the surplus in fixed-temperature mode. In heat-balanced mode the bath temperature is the root of f(T) between 800 and 2000 °C, with the equilibrium recomputed at every trial temperature. For display, Qnet is split into sensible terms relative to T0 and chemical terms Qg for the element groups g: coal (C, H, S), Al, Zn, Pb, Fe and Si. Qmelt is the heat needed to bring cold charge from its crystalline state to the slag standard state.

Batch cycle

In step k the bath left by the previous step is combined with the feeds of one time increment Δt and brought to equilibrium. The gas leaves at the bath temperature Tk and the condensed phases form the next bath. In heat-balanced mode the step energy balance gives Tk. Recovery counts the zinc vapour leaving the bath. Direct CO2 assumes that all coal carbon ends as CO2 after post-combustion.

Two-zone option

A share φ of the blast does not meet the slag. The bath equilibrates with the remaining blast and releases gas A. Gas A then reacts with the bypassed blast to a gas-only equilibrium B at the bath temperature. A fraction η of the heat released returns to the bath.
