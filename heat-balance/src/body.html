What the model does

For a given set of feeds the model finds the phase assemblage with the lowest total Gibbs energy at the bath temperature and pressure, subject to the element balance. The enthalpy of that assemblage, compared with the enthalpy of the feeds, gives the heat balance. In heat-balanced mode the bath temperature is the one at which the two agree after the heat loss is taken out.

  • Batch cycle. The bath is brought to equilibrium with each time increment of blast, coal and aluminium. The gas then leaves and the next increment meets the remaining bath. Cold charge is fed evenly over the cycle.
  • Continuous. One well-mixed stage. Product slag and off-gas leave in equilibrium with each other.

Phases and species

  • Gas (ideal): N2, O2, CO, CO2, H2, H2O, CH4, Zn, Pb, PbO, PbS, SO2, H2S, S2, COS.
  • Slag: solution of FeO, FeO1.5, ZnO, PbO, SiO2, CaO, AlO1.5, MgO on a single-cation mole fraction basis. ZnO, PbO, FeO and FeO1.5 carry a constant activity coefficient that you set. The other oxides are treated as ideal diluents. Sulfur does not dissolve in the slag.
  • Pure condensed phases: iron, lead, graphite, magnetite, and aluminium and silicon as sinks for excess reductant.

Standard states and data

  • Thermochemical functions are the NASA Glenn polynomials (McBride, Gordon and Reno, NASA TM-4513, 1993), read from the Cantera 3.2 data files. Outside a fitted range the heat capacity is held constant.
  • ZnO is not in that file. It uses the CODATA values at 298.15 K (ΔfH° = −350.46 kJ/mol, S° = 43.65 J/(mol·K)) with Cp = 45.338 + 7.289×10−3T − 5.732×105T−2 J/(mol·K). These are the data of the slag-fuming-model repository, which records agreement with SUPCRT16 zincite within 1.3 kJ.
  • Slag standard states: liquid FeO, PbO and SiO2; solid ZnO, CaO, MgO, α-Al2O3 and hematite. Activity coefficients must be given on the same basis. For ZnO, PbO, FeO and FeO1.5 the standard states and the mole fraction basis are the same as in the slag-fuming-model repository, so activity coefficients fitted there carry over as long as copper and cadmium oxides are minor.
  • The JANAF FeO is the stoichiometric compound and is more stable than iron oxide in equilibrium with iron. Its Gibbs energy is raised by 5829.5 + 3.1911T J/mol, which is 10.7 kJ/mol at 1250 °C. This is the correction used in the slag-fuming-model repository, taken there from the Calphad Fe–O description. An independent estimate from Fe + ½O2 = FeO(s), ΔG° = −263 700 + 64.35T J, with the JANAF fusion data gives about 8.1 kJ/mol. The difference moves the FeO activity scale by a factor of about 1.2 and is absorbed by γ FeO.
  • Coal enters through its dry ultimate analysis and higher heating value. If you let the model estimate the heating value it uses the Channiwala and Parikh (2002) correlation. Ash is carried as 60 % SiO2 and 40 % Al2O3.

Solver and checks

The minimisation is a Lagrangian Newton iteration on the element potentials (the RAND method used in NASA CEA), extended with a second solution phase and with rules for adding and removing phases. At every solution the chemical potential of each species equals the sum of its element potentials to better than 10−9 in μ/RT. In 44 000 randomised cases the solver converged every time. Thirteen cases were compared with an independent SciPy minimisation using Cantera to evaluate the species data. The minimum G/RT agreed within 5×10−8 in all thirteen and within 3×10−9 in eleven, which is the level of the SciPy element balance residual. The heat and mass balances shown here close to rounding error by construction.

Limits you should keep in mind

  • The result is an equilibrium limit for one well-mixed zone. Richards, Brimacombe and Toop (1985) showed that a tuyere-blown fuming furnace behaves as a reduction zone around entrained coal and an oxidation zone in the tuyere gas column. The optional setting under Blast lets a share of the blast burn the bath gas without meeting the slag. Use it to calibrate against plant data. It is zero by default.
  • Constant activity coefficients are a coarse slag model. Published values for ZnO scatter widely. The defaults are placeholders in the reported range. Calibrate them against FactSage, ChemApp or measured end-of-cycle assays for your slag.
  • Slag enthalpy is the sum of the component oxides. Heats of mixing, including the heat of dissolving Al2O3 from aluminium oxidation, are neglected.
  • Liquidus, viscosity and kinetics are not modelled. The model will report a bath temperature at which the slag would not be liquid.
  • Metallic iron is pure iron. No matte, speiss or alloy phase is included.
