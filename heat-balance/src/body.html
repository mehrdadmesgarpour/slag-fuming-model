External thermodynamic data

Imported data replace the built-in standard states species by species and are used in both the Gibbs energy minimisation and the heat balance. Values must be in J/mol and J/(mol K), with enthalpies relative to the elements at 298.15 K (the SER convention of Calphad and the formation-enthalpy convention of NASA and JANAF) and absolute entropies. Gas data are converted to the model's 1 bar standard pressure.

What the model does

For a given set of feeds the model finds the phase assemblage with the lowest total Gibbs energy at the bath temperature and pressure, subject to the element balance. The enthalpy of that assemblage, compared with the enthalpy of the feeds, gives the heat balance. In heat-balanced mode the bath temperature is the one at which the two agree after the heat loss is taken out.

  • Batch cycle. The bath is brought to equilibrium with each time increment of blast, coal and aluminium. The gas then leaves and the next increment meets the remaining bath. Cold charge is fed evenly over the cycle.
  • Continuous. One well-mixed stage. Product slag and off-gas leave in equilibrium with each other.

Furnace cell model

The Furnace tab resolves the batch cycle in space. A vertical cross-section of the bath is divided into cells. Slag circulates between them in two loops that rise at the side walls. Blast, coal and aluminium enter at the tuyere cells, and the gas rises cell by cell to the top. In every time step every cell is solved by the same Gibbs energy minimisation and heat balance as the rest of the page, with the pressure raised by the slag above it. The equations are in the section "Furnace cell model" on the Formulas tab. Clicking a cell shows every equation used for it with its numbers: the element balance, the heat balance and the temperature iterations, the Newton iterations with the element potentials, the chemical potential of every species, and the bath equilibria.

The time step is limited so that a cell exchanges at most 80 % of its slag per step, which is usually shorter than the batch time step on the left. With two cells, no circulation and no bath depth the cell model reproduces the well-mixed batch cycle (zinc recovery 72.1775 % against 72.1776 %). On every grid tested the element and energy balances of the whole furnace close to 10−13 of the input. The circulation pattern, the share of gas that moves inward and the share of coal carried up the wall are prescribed, not computed from fluid flow. Use them to explore the effect of mixing, or set them from CFD results.

Phases and species

  • Gas (ideal, standard pressure 1 bar): N2, O2, CO, CO2, H2, H2O, CH4, Zn, Pb, PbO, PbS, SO2, H2S, S2, COS. Radicals such as O, OH and H are left out. They change the adiabatic flame temperature of CO in air by 14 K but are negligible at bath temperatures.
  • Slag: solution of FeO, FeO1.5, ZnO, PbO, SiO2, CaO, AlO1.5, MgO on a single-cation mole fraction basis. ZnO, PbO, FeO and FeO1.5 carry activity coefficients: constant, regular-solution, a temperature table, or values from a database server. The other oxides are ideal diluents. Sulfur does not dissolve in the slag.
  • Pure condensed phases: iron, lead, graphite, magnetite, liquid FeS as a matte, and aluminium and silicon as sinks for excess reductant.
  • Saturation checks outside the minimisation: CaS (a sign that sulfur would go into the slag as sulfide) and liquid zinc (possible at low temperature and high pressure). The page warns when either activity exceeds 1.

Standard states and data

  • Thermochemical functions are the NASA Glenn polynomials (McBride, Gordon and Reno, NASA TM-4513, 1993), read from the Cantera 3.2 data files. They refer to 1 bar. Cantera itself reads these files as 1 atm data because the files do not state a pressure; the model uses 1 bar, which the tabulated entropies confirm. Outside a fitted range the heat capacity is held constant. At bath temperature this applies to liquid FeO (fitted from 1650 K) and liquid SiO2 (from 1696 K).
  • ZnO is not in that file. It uses the CODATA values at 298.15 K (ΔfH° = −350.46 kJ/mol, S° = 43.65 J/(mol·K)) with Cp = 45.338 + 7.289×10−3T − 5.732×105T−2 J/(mol·K). These are the data of the slag-fuming-model repository, which records agreement with SUPCRT16 zincite within 1.3 kJ.
  • Slag standard states: liquid FeO, PbO and SiO2; solid ZnO, CaO, MgO, α-Al2O3 and hematite. Activity coefficients must be given on the same basis. For ZnO, PbO, FeO and FeO1.5 the standard states and the mole fraction basis are the same as in the slag-fuming-model repository.
  • The JANAF FeO is the stoichiometric compound and is more stable than iron oxide in equilibrium with iron. Its Gibbs energy is raised by 5829.5 + 3.1911T J/mol, 10.7 kJ/mol at 1250 °C (correction of the slag-fuming-model repository, from the Calphad Fe–O description). An independent estimate from Fe + ½O2 = FeO(s), ΔG° = −263 700 + 64.35T J, with the JANAF fusion data gives about 8.1 kJ/mol. The difference moves the FeO activity scale by a factor of about 1.2 and is absorbed by γ FeO.
  • Coal enters through its dry ultimate analysis and higher heating value. If you let the model estimate the heating value it uses the Channiwala and Parikh (2002) correlation. Ash enters with its analysis (SiO2, Al2O3, CaO, Fe2O3, MgO) as crystalline oxides at 25 °C.
  • Any of these records can be replaced from the Database tab: by file (JSON, NASA-7, NASA-9, Cantera YAML, Calphad TDB, CSV) or from a local server that reads TDB, Cantera or JSON data and can evaluate a slag solution model.

Solver

The minimisation is a Lagrangian Newton iteration on the element potentials (the RAND method used in NASA CEA), extended with a second solution phase and with rules for adding and removing phases. A phase set that repeats switches the rules from adding first to removing first. A gas phase that consists only of vapours of pure condensed phases makes the Newton matrix singular, so the solver then restarts without it and lets the saturation test decide.

Verification

  • Independent solver. Cantera's VCS and Gibbs multiphase solvers, given the same species data, reach the same minimum within 5×10−7 in G/RT and 10−6 in the species amounts in 13 cases from oxidising to strongly over-reduced. A SciPy minimisation started from perturbed points agrees within 4×10−9 in all 13.
  • Robustness. 44 000 randomised cases over the full input range and 6000 cases at low temperature and high pressure converged. In every case the chemical potential of each species equals the sum of its element potentials to better than 10−9 in μ/RT.
  • Data. The polynomial evaluation reproduces Cantera within 10−4 J/mol. The reaction relations on the Formulas tab hold on solved states to 10−14.
  • Heat balance. Adiabatic flame temperatures of CO and CH4 in air agree with Cantera within 0.5 K, which is the 1 bar against 1 atm labelling above. The heat and mass balances close to 10−9 MW in every mode, with constant, regular and tabulated activity coefficients, post-combustion and cold charge.
  • Time step. Halving the batch time step from 2 to 1 min changes zinc recovery by 0.1 percentage points. The limit for small steps is about 0.25 points above the 2 min result.
  • External data. Built-in data written in each file format and read back are reproduced exactly (CSV tables within 0.005 J/mol). Through the local server, activity coefficients from a Calphad regular-solution slag evaluated by pycalphad match the analytic values within 10−5.

Limits you should keep in mind

  • The result is an equilibrium limit for one well-mixed zone. Richards, Brimacombe and Toop (1985) showed that a tuyere-blown fuming furnace behaves as a reduction zone around entrained coal and an oxidation zone in the tuyere gas column. The setting under Blast lets a share of the blast burn the bath gas without meeting the slag. Use it to calibrate against plant data. It is zero by default.
  • The built-in activity coefficients are constants. γ ZnO (2.1) and γ FeO1.5 (4.3), together with the default coal heating value (31.4 MJ/kg), were fitted to a reference calculation for the default case. They are not fitted to plant data. Zinc recovery is very sensitive to γ ZnO. Calibrate them, or link a database with a slag solution model and iterate.
  • The slag enthalpy is that of the component oxides plus the excess enthalpy of the four oxides with activity coefficients. For a fayalite composition, the heat to take cold slag to liquid at 1250 °C is 1.36 MJ/kg in the model, against 1.54 MJ/kg from the measured data of Stebbins and Carmichael (1984). Heat demand for cold charge is therefore about 12 % low.
  • Sulfur has no sulfide capacity in the slag. When the page reports CaS saturation, real slag would hold sulfur that the model sends to the gas.
  • Liquidus, viscosity and kinetics are not modelled. The model will report a bath temperature at which the slag would not be liquid.
  • Metallic iron is pure iron and the matte is pure FeS. No alloy or speiss phase is included.
